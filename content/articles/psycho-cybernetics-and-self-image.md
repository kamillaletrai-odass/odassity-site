Maxwell Maltz spent his career as a plastic surgeon fixing faces. He found the problem was never the face — patients who hated their reflection before surgery often hated it just as much after, if their self-image hadn't moved.

Swap "plastic surgeon" for "For You Page" and the theory gets uncomfortably current. Filters, angles, and edits promise the same fix Maltz's patients were chasing, and run into the same wall: the image in the mirror isn't the one that needs the update.

An autopsy of *Psycho-Cybernetics*, six decades later, applied to a feed instead of a face.
